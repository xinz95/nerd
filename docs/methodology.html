NERD Baseball Watchability
Today's Games Pitchers Teams Methodology

Methodology

NERD is a numerical estimate of the watchability of a given MLB game on a given day. It is inspired by Carson Cistulli's NERD at FanGraphs. Each game receives a score from 0–10. A score of 5 represents a league-average game. Higher is more watchable.

Game NERD

A game's score is a weighted combination of the two starting pitchers' scores and the two teams' scores:

Game NERD = (pNERDaway + pNERDhome) / 2 × 0.6  +  (tNERDaway + tNERDhome) / 2 × 0.4

Clamped to [0, 10].

The 60/40 split reflects the view that the pitching matchup is the primary driver of watchability — a marquee ace duel is more compelling than the same two teams with replacement-level starters.

Pitcher NERD (pNERD)

Each starting pitcher is scored on four components. Each component is z-scored across all qualified starting pitchers league-wide for the current season, then the weighted sum is mapped to the 0–10 scale.

Component Direction Weight Why it matters
xFIP lower = better (inverted) 30% Expected Fielding Independent Pitching — measures strikeouts, walks, and HR rate, normalizing HR/FB to league average. The best single predictor of true pitching quality.
K% higher = better 25% Strikeout rate (strikeouts / batters faced). Preferred over K/9 because it is not inflated by pitchers who allow lots of hits — every batter faced is treated equally. High-strikeout pitchers create more individual confrontations and are inherently more watchable.
BB% lower = better (inverted) 20% Walk rate (walks / batters faced). Preferred over BB/9 for the same reason as K%. Free passes kill pace and tension.
Pitch Quality higher = better 25% A composite of three pitch-level components derived from play-by-play data for the pitcher's last 5 starts. Each sub-component is z-scored at the league level before combining:
  • Velocity (45%) — avg fastball speed (mph)
  • Induced vertical break (35%) — avg "rise" on the fastball (inches). Measures how much the ball rises above a spinless trajectory — a proxy for backspin quality and carry.
  • Absolute horizontal break (20%) — avg lateral movement (inches, sign-agnostic). More arm-side run or cut generally means harder to square up.
Fastball family: 4-seam (FF), 2-seam (FT), sinker (SI), cutter (FC).
zPQ = z(velocity) × 0.45 + z(IVB) × 0.35 + z(|HB|) × 0.20
compositez = −z(xFIP) × 0.30 + z(K%) × 0.25 −z(BB%) × 0.20 + zPQ × 0.25
pNERD = 5 + clamp(compositez, −3, 3) / 3 × 5

All z-scores are computed over all pitchers meeting the innings threshold (see Qualifying Thresholds). Clamping at ±3 standard deviations prevents extreme outliers from compressing everyone else.

Fallback: FIP

If xFIP is not available for a pitcher, FIP is substituted. The card is flagged FIP used.

Team NERD (tNERD)

Each team is scored on four components, z-scored across all 30 MLB teams and mapped to 0–10. tNERD captures whether a team is interesting to watch independent of today's starter.

Component Direction Weight Why it matters
wRC+ higher = better 35% Weighted Runs Created Plus — measures overall offensive production relative to league average (100 = average). Offensively explosive teams are more watchable.
Proxy ERA lower = better (inverted) 30% Runs allowed per 9 innings (from season-to-date standings). Strong pitching staffs suppress scoring and create more tension.
Pythagorean Luck closer to 0 = better (inverted) 20% Actual win% minus Pythagorean win% (RS1.83 / (RS1.83 + RA1.83)). A team significantly overperforming its run differential is due for regression — less interesting to watch. Underperformers have upside. Teams near 0 are playing as expected.
Run Differential / G closer to 0 = better (inverted) 15% Absolute run differential per game. Lopsided teams (very dominant or very bad) make for less competitive games. Teams near zero tend to be in close contests.
compositez = z(wRC+) × 0.35 − z(Proxy ERA) × 0.30 − z(Luck) × 0.20 − z(|RD/G|) × 0.15
tNERD = 5 + clamp(compositez, −3, 3) / 3 × 5

wRC+ is computed as the median wRC+ across all qualified batters on the team (minimum 10 games). Proxy ERA and run differential come directly from current standings.

Early-Season Blending

In the first 30 games of the season, small sample sizes make current-year stats unreliable. To compensate, pitcher stats are blended with prior-season stats using a linear weight based on how many starts the pitcher has logged in the current season.

w = min(1, startscurrent / 5)
blended stat = statcurrent × w + statprior × (1 − w)

At 0 current starts: 100% prior season. At 5+ starts: 100% current season. Linear blend in between. Blending applies to xFIP, FIP, K/9, and BB/9. Fastball velocity follows the same logic: if no current-year velocity data exists, the prior season's average is used.

Pitcher situationResultFlag shown
0 starts this season, has prior MLB stats 100% prior-year stats 2025 stats
1–4 starts this season Linear blend of current + prior blended stats
5+ starts this season 100% current-year stats —
No prior MLB stats (debut, NPB/KBO signing, etc.) pNERD = 5.0 (league average) limited data
After game 30 of the season Current-year stats only, no blending —

Team changes are handled automatically: prior-year stats are individual (not team-attributed), so a pitcher who moved teams carries their 2025 numbers to their 2026 team without any manual correction needed. tNERD is always computed from current-season standings and is unaffected by the blending.

Qualifying Thresholds

To be included in the z-score population (and thus receive a meaningful pNERD rather than the 5.0 default), a pitcher must meet a minimum innings pitched threshold. This threshold scales with the season:

min IP = max(3, avg games played per team)

Early in April this is ~3–7 IP. By mid-season it rises to ~30+ IP. This ensures the z-score population is never too thin to be meaningful. Pitchers below the threshold receive pNERD = 5.0 and a limited data flag.

Pitch Quality Calculation

Pitch quality data is not available as a season-level aggregate from the MLB Stats API. It is computed by fetching pitch-by-pitch play-by-play data for the pitcher's last 5 starts and averaging these fields across all fastball-family pitches:

FieldAPI keyDescription
VelocitypitchData.startSpeedRelease speed in mph
Induced vertical breakpitchData.breaks.breakVerticalInducedInches of rise above a spinless trajectory
Horizontal breakpitchData.breaks.breakHorizontalLateral movement in inches (absolute value used)

Fastball types included: FF (4-seam), FT (2-seam), SI (sinker), FC (cutter).

Results are cached in localStorage for 1 hour. During early season blending, if a pitcher has no current-year starts, pitch quality data is pulled from their prior-season games instead.

Limitations

  • Early season noise. Even with prior-year blending, scores in April reflect limited data. Treat scores as directional rather than precise until ~May.
  • No ballpark adjustment. Playing at Coors Field or Petco Park affects run scoring, but tNERD does not currently park-adjust its proxy ERA or wRC+.
  • No bullpen component. A great starter matched with a terrible bullpen is still scored on the starter alone. Bullpen quality is partially captured in team proxy ERA but not isolated.
  • Debut players receive 5.0. Players with no prior MLB record (international signings, call-ups) have insufficient data for a meaningful score and default to league average.
  • TBD starters receive 5.0. When a probable pitcher hasn't been announced, both the pitcher score and its pNERD contribution default to league average.
