NERD Baseball Watchability
Today's Games Pitchers Teams Methodology

Methodology

NERD is a numerical estimate of the watchability of a given MLB game on a given day. It is inspired by Carson Cistulli's NERD at FanGraphs. Each game receives a score from 0–10. A score of 5 represents a league-average game. Higher is more watchable.

Game NERD

A game's score is a weighted combination of the two starting pitchers' scores and the two teams' scores:

Game NERD = (pNERDaway + pNERDhome) / 2 × 0.6  +  (tNERDaway + tNERDhome) / 2 × 0.4

Clamped to [0, 10].

The 60/40 split reflects the view that the pitching matchup is the primary driver of watchability — a marquee ace duel is more compelling than the same two teams with replacement-level starters.

Pitcher NERD (pNERD)

Each starting pitcher is scored on four components. Each component is z-scored across all qualified starting pitchers league-wide for the current season, then the weighted sum is mapped to the 0–10 scale.

Component Direction Weight Why it matters
xFIP lower = better (inverted) 30% Expected Fielding Independent Pitching — measures strikeouts, walks, and HR rate, normalizing HR/FB to league average. The best single predictor of true pitching quality.
K%−BB% higher = better 40% Strikeout rate minus walk rate (both measured per batter faced). Combining them into a single spread metric captures the two sides of pitcher control in one number — elite strikeout ability and elite command pull in the same direction, while poor command partially offsets a high K%. Rate stats are preferred over K/9 and BB/9 because they are not distorted by hit rates.
Pitch Quality higher = better 30% A composite of two pitch-level components derived from play-by-play data for the pitcher's last 5 starts. Each sub-component is z-scored at the league level before combining:
  • Velocity (50%) — avg fastball speed (mph)
  • Whiff rate (50%) — swinging strikes / total swings across all pitch types. Directly measures swing-and-miss ability, the clearest signal of stuff quality.
IVB, |HB|, and spin efficiency are shown on the Pitchers page as context but are not included in the formula.
zPQ = z(velocity) × 0.50 + z(whiff%) × 0.50
compositez = −z(xFIP) × 0.30 + z(K%−BB%) × 0.40 + zPQ × 0.30
pNERD = 5 + clamp(compositez, −2, 2) / 2 × 5

All z-scores are computed over qualifying pitchers (see Qualifying Thresholds). The weighted composite is then re-z-scored across the population before mapping to 0–10, ensuring scores always spread across the full range rather than clustering near 5. Clamping at ±2σ prevents extreme outliers from compressing everyone else.

Fallback: FIP

If xFIP is not available for a pitcher, FIP is substituted. The card is flagged FIP used.

Team NERD (tNERD)

Each team is scored on six components, z-scored across all 30 MLB teams and mapped to 0–10. tNERD captures whether a team is interesting to watch independent of today's starter.

Component Direction Weight Why it matters
wRC+ higher = better 30% Weighted Runs Created Plus — measures overall offensive production relative to league average (100 = average). Offensively explosive teams are more watchable.
Proxy ERA lower = better (inverted) 12.5% Runs allowed per 9 innings (from season-to-date standings). Strong pitching staffs suppress scoring and create more tension.
Bullpen ERA lower = better (inverted) 12.5% ERA of relief pitchers specifically. A strong bullpen protects leads and makes late-inning situations more compelling.
Pythagorean Luck higher = better 20% Actual win% minus Pythagorean win% (RS1.83 / (RS1.83 + RA1.83)). A team outperforming its run differential is playing winning baseball and is more compelling to watch.
Run Differential / G closer to 0 = better (inverted) 15% Absolute run differential per game. Lopsided teams (very dominant or very bad) make for less competitive games. Teams near zero tend to be in close contests.
Batting K% lower = better (inverted) 10% Team strikeout rate as batters (strikeouts / plate appearances). High-strikeout lineups produce fewer balls in play and less action. Contact-heavy teams create more baserunners and field events.
compositez = z(wRC+) × 0.30 − z(pERA) × 0.125 − z(bpERA) × 0.125 + z(Luck) × 0.20 − z(|RD/G|) × 0.15 − z(K%) × 0.10
tNERD = 5 + clamp(compositez, −2, 2) / 2 × 5

wRC+ and K% are both PA-weighted across all batters on the team (minimum 10 PA). Proxy ERA and run differential come directly from current standings.

Early-Season Handling

During March and April, current-season sample sizes are too small to be meaningful. Through the end of April, prior-year stats are used wholesale for both pNERD and tNERD — no blending. Starting May 1, all scores switch to current-season stats only.

PeriodStats usedFlag shown on cards
March & April (month ≤ 4) Prior-year stats (pNERD & tNERD) 2025 stats
May 1 onward Current-season stats only —
No prior MLB stats (debut, NPB/KBO signing, etc.) pNERD = 7.0 (unknown upside) limited data

Prior-year stats are individual (not team-attributed), so a pitcher who changed teams carries their prior numbers to their new team without any manual correction.

Qualifying Thresholds

To appear on the pitchers list and receive a meaningful pNERD score, a starter must have made at least 10 games started in the active stats season. This filters out spot starters while still capturing full-time starters who missed time.

Pitchers with no prior MLB record (debuts, international signings) are not present in the stats data and receive pNERD = 5.0 (league average) with a limited data flag.

Pitch Quality Calculation

Pitch quality data is not available as a season-level aggregate from the MLB Stats API. It is computed by fetching pitch-by-pitch play-by-play data for the pitcher's last 5 starts:

FieldAPI keyDescription
VelocitypitchData.startSpeedRelease speed in mph — fastball-family pitches only (FF, FT, SI, FC)
Whiff ratedetails.codeSwinging strikes (codes S, W) / total swings (S, W, F, T, L, O, M, X, D, E) across all pitch types
Induced vertical breakpitchData.breaks.breakVerticalInducedInches of rise above a spinless trajectory (display only)
Horizontal breakpitchData.breaks.breakHorizontalLateral movement in inches, absolute value (display only)
Spin ratepitchData.breaks.spinRateRPM — used to compute spin efficiency (display only)

Results are cached in localStorage for 1 hour. During early season (March/April), pitch data is pulled from the prior year's games, consistent with the rest of the stats.

Limitations

  • Early season. Through April, scores are based entirely on prior-year stats — they reflect last season's performance, not this year's. New-team fits, injuries, and development aren't captured until May.
  • No ballpark adjustment. Playing at Coors Field or Petco Park affects run scoring, but tNERD does not currently park-adjust its proxy ERA or wRC+.
  • Bullpen quality is team-level only. Bullpen ERA is captured in tNERD, but individual relief pitchers are not scored. A great starter matched with a shaky bullpen will have a high pNERD regardless.
  • Debut players receive 7.0. Players with no prior MLB record (international signings, call-ups) have insufficient data for a meaningful score. They're given a slight above-average score to reflect the unknown upside of a new arm.
  • TBD starters receive 5.0. When a probable pitcher hasn't been announced, both the pitcher score and its pNERD contribution default to league average.
